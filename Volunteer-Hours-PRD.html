01
Problem Frame

The framework: volunteer-hour tracking is a capture-then-report problem, not a data problem. Value is lost at the two ends — friction at capture (hours never get logged) and friction at report (totals are painful to assemble). Everything in this product optimizes those two moments.

Today the hours live in a Google Sheet, an Excel file, or a person's memory. Each of those fails predictably. Spreadsheets demand a laptop, a correct row, and a running formula the user has to trust. Memory doesn't survive a week. The cost isn't dramatic on any single day — it's the slow erosion of an incomplete record that surfaces exactly when an accurate total is needed (an organization's report, a scholarship, a service requirement).

Core insight — the tool that wins here isn't the one with the most features. It's the one that makes logging today's hours a five-second act on the device already in the user's hand, and makes "how many hours this quarter?" a one-tap answer.

The smallest version that delivers real value: open the app, confirm project and date, type a number, save. Then ask a period and read a total. If v1 nails those two loops, it has already beaten the spreadsheet.

02
User & Context
Primary user
The individual volunteer
One person, one device, no account. Logs hours across a handful of recurring projects and periodically needs a defensible total to report to an organization.
Where
On phone, in the moment
Entry happens right after a shift — in a car, on a couch, walking out of a building. Often one-handed, often in a hurry. The context is mobile-first, not desk-first.
Why now
The spreadsheet is friction
The current tools require a laptop or a good memory. A purpose-built, single-tap logger removes both dependencies and makes the record complete.
Jobs to be done
J1
"Log the hours I just did" — with the least possible friction, so the record actually gets kept.
J2
"Tell me my total for a period" — this week, month, quarter, or calendar year, on demand.
J3
"Show me what I logged and when" — a trustworthy running record I can scan and correct.
J4
"Get the numbers out" — into a CSV or Google Sheet when an organization needs a report.
03
Evidence & Assumptions

This is a personal-utility build, so the "evidence" is the stated behavior of the current workflow plus a small set of named assumptions. Naming them is the point — hidden assumptions are how good reasoning fails in practice.

Signals we trust
Hours are currently kept in Sheets / Excel / memory — confirmed.

Projects are few and recurring, so a preset-plus-custom list will cover almost every entry.

Reporting need is periodic and period-based (week / month / quarter / year), not continuous analytics.
Assumptions to validate
A1 — One decimal of hours (e.g. 2.5) is precise enough; no minute-level timers needed.

A2 — A single device is sufficient; the user won't need multi-device sync in v1.

A3 — Google Sheets export is worth a one-time Google authorization (see Open Questions).

05
Scope & Constraints
IN v1.0 (MVP)
  • Log hours: project (last-used default), date (today default), quarter-hour increments, freeform notes
  • History table with running total; edit & delete entries
  • Reports: week / month / quarter / calendar year, with per-project breakdown
  • Calendar: day-by-day view + summarized window totals; marks only days with entries
  • Projects: preset list + custom add, rename, archive
  • Local on-device storage as the source of truth (the only store)
  • CSV export handed to the native Android share sheet
OUT Not in v1.0
  • Accounts, login, or multi-user / organization roles
  • Cloud sync across multiple devices
  • Live timers / clock-in-clock-out capture
  • Goals, reminders, streaks, or notifications
  • iOS or web clients (Android-only for v1)
  • In-app analytics dashboards beyond the four period totals
  • Direct Google Sheets / OAuth integration (share sheet covers it)
  • Cloud backup of the local store (deferred, not designed out)
Technical
Native Android. Local persistence (e.g. Room/SQLite). Data model designed so a sync layer can be added later without a migration rewrite.
Design
Follows the Sage personal design system — one accent, white space as structure, hierarchy over decoration. Sage #7EC8BB for interactive elements only.
Privacy
No accounts, no network in the MVP means no data leaves the device unless the user explicitly exports. Minimal attack surface by design.
06
Non-Functional & Downstream

What this touches, not just what it solves. Every decision here has a blast radius — mapped below.

01
Data durability is the real risk. Local-only storage means a lost or wiped phone loses the record — the exact failure we're trying to fix. Mitigation: make export easy and obvious, and treat cloud backup as the top post-MVP candidate.
02
Privacy is a feature, not an afterthought. No login and no network keep the data on-device and the attack surface near zero. Any future sync flips this — it introduces auth, a backend, and a real privacy surface, and should be a deliberate, documented decision.
03
Export stays offline by design. CSV to the native share sheet keeps the app free of Google OAuth, backend, and network permissions — the user's OS handles where the file goes. Play Store data-safety disclosure stays trivially simple: no data collected, no data shared.
04
Performance is trivial at this scale. A single volunteer's entries are small data; local queries are instant. Design for clarity, not throughput.

DecisionRationale & tradeoff
Local-only storage DECIDEDConfirmed sufficient for v1. Maximizes privacy and simplicity, zero accounts, no network. Durability trade accepted, mitigated by easy CSV export; cloud backup deferred, not designed out.
Single user, no loginMatches the actual user and removes an entire auth/backend surface from v1. Multi-user is a different product, out of scope.
Preset + custom projectsBalances zero-setup start against clean, de-duplicated data. Chosen over free-text (messy) and fixed presets (too rigid).
CSV via Android share sheet DECIDEDThe sole export path for v1. Export builds a CSV and hands it to the native Android share sheet (email, Drive, Sheets, Files — user's choice). No auth, no network, no Google dependency. Direct Google Sheets write deferred.
Android-only v1Focus. Cross-platform is deferred, not designed out — the data model stays portable.
